How Much Revenue Does Your Clinic Lose to Missed Calls?

Published July 25, 2026 · 8 min read

Every clinic answers most of its calls. That's not the problem. The problem is the ones that slip through — the call that arrives during a packed morning, the one that comes in at 6:40pm after the front desk has clocked out, the one that hits voicemail on a Saturday. Individually, each of those feels like a small, forgivable miss. Added up over a month, they're often the single biggest source of lost revenue a clinic never actually measures.

This post walks through where that revenue actually goes, gives you the exact formula to calculate your own number, and outlines what's realistic to do about it.

Why missed calls cost more than they seem to

A missed call doesn't just cost you that one appointment. It costs you the appointment and the patient's willingness to try calling back. Healthcare consumer research consistently shows that patients who reach voicemail or an unanswered line don't wait around — they call the next clinic on their list. For a private practice competing with three or four other options in the same zip code, that's not a hypothetical; it's the default outcome.

Three specific gaps tend to account for most of the loss:

  • Peak-hour overflow — mornings and lunch, when call volume outpaces available staff
  • After-hours calls — evenings, weekends, holidays, when the clinic is simply closed but patients are still calling
  • No follow-up on missed calls — even when a call is missed, most clinics have no system that reaches back out automatically

The formula

You don't need complex analytics to get a reasonable estimate. Here's the calculation, broken into its actual components:

missedCallsPerWeek = weeklyCalls × (missedPct / 100)
unhandledPerWeek = missedCallsPerWeek + afterHoursCalls
lostBookingsPerWeek = unhandledPerWeek × (bookingConversionRate / 100)
lostBookingsPerMonth = lostBookingsPerWeek × 4.33
monthlyRevenueLoss = lostBookingsPerMonth × avgAppointmentValue

The logic behind each step: start with how many calls you're missing during business hours, add every after-hours call (which is unhandled by definition), then apply the same booking conversion rate you'd expect from a call that actually gets answered — because not every unhandled call would have converted anyway. Multiply by 4.33 (the average number of weeks in a month) to annualize, then multiply by what an average appointment is worth to your clinic.

A worked example

Here's what this looks like for a mid-sized dental practice — 140 calls a week, a 20% missed-call rate during business hours, 15 after-hours calls a week, a $150 average appointment value, and a 35% booking conversion rate on calls that do get answered:

After-hours calls15/week

Lost bookings~15/week

Estimated monthly loss~$9,750

That's not an edge case — it's a fairly ordinary clinic with fairly ordinary call volume. The number surprises most owners not because the math is unusual, but because nobody was tracking it in the first place.

What to actually do about it

The fix isn't necessarily "hire another front desk person." Adding headcount solves the peak-hour overflow problem but does nothing for the after-hours gap — clinics don't typically staff a receptionist at 9pm on a Tuesday. The three gaps above map fairly directly to three practical fixes:

  1. Peak-hour overflow → an overflow system that picks up calls the front desk can't get to in time
  2. After-hours calls → 24/7 coverage, whether that's an answering service or an AI receptionist
  3. No follow-up → an automatic text-back system so a missed call still gets a response within minutes, not never

Whatever the fix, the first step is the same: measure it. Most clinics have never actually run this calculation for their own numbers — they're going on a gut feeling instead of a figure.
