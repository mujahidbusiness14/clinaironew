How Much Does an AI Receptionist Cost? A Realistic Pricing Breakdown

Published July 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Pricing for AI receptionist tools varies more than most buyers expect, and the differences aren't always obvious from a pricing page. Here's what actually drives the cost, and what to watch for before committing.

The three common pricing models

  • Flat monthly rate — one predictable price regardless of call volume. Easiest to budget for, but check what's actually included at that tier.
  • Per-minute or per-call — costs scale with usage. Can be cheaper for low-volume clinics, but unpredictable during busy months (which, notably, is exactly when you need the coverage most).
  • Tiered by feature set — basic answering at one price, scheduling/CRM sync/reminders bundled at a higher tier.

What tends to be underpriced or hidden

Watch for these add-on costs that aren't always visible on the initial pricing page:

  • Setup/onboarding fees — separate from the monthly rate, sometimes substantial
  • Per-integration charges — connecting to your specific CRM or EHR may cost extra
  • Overage charges — exceeding a call-volume cap on usage-based plans
  • Multi-location pricing — some tools charge per-location, which adds up quickly for group practices

What a realistic range looks like

For a single-location private clinic, all-in monthly costs (including setup amortized over time) for a genuinely capable AI front desk system — voice answering, scheduling, and basic automation — typically land somewhere in the mid-hundreds to low-thousands per month, depending on feature scope. Tools quoting far below that range are usually offering answering-only, without real scheduling or CRM integration. Tools quoting far above it are often enterprise platforms built for hospital systems, not private practices.

The cost comparison that actually matters

The right comparison isn't "AI receptionist cost" vs. "$0" — it's AI receptionist cost vs. the cost of a part-time front desk hire (wages, training, turnover, and the hours they still can't cover) vs. the revenue currently lost to missed and after-hours calls. Run your own numbers rather than comparing sticker prices in isolation.
